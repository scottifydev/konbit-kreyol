# 01 · WORLD AND STORY — the fiction and the history bible

**The history in this document is fact-checked (2026-07-10, sources at the end) and binding: dates, people, spellings, and legend-vs-documented flags must not drift in any scene, card, or check. Kreyòl spellings follow IPN orthography; flagged items await the native reviewer. Every "the story goes" flag is load-bearing: the game must not present tradition as documented fact.**

---

## 1. Premise

You and your brother are couriers and signal-runners in the revolutionary army, 1791–1804. The revolution runs on communication the enemy cannot read: **Kreyòl is the weapon**. Orders, warnings, songs, and proverbs move through the network — and the network is a group chat on your phone, because in this world the revolution has phones. Nobody explains this. Nobody apologizes for it.

The fiction is second-person, present tense, text-based, with choices, checks, battles — and above all **voice**: the messages that matter are spoken. A rider stops you at the ford. He speaks fast: « Kote w prale? » Your brother is holding the other end of the line. What you say into your phone, he has to understand.

## 2. The Anachronism Doctrine (summary — the law lives in `04-laws.md`)

1. **Medium, never history.** Phones, the feed, texting, anthems exist in-world. Every date, person, event, spelling, and legend-flag obeys this document. History checks never test anachronistic content as fact.
2. **Never explained, never apologized for.** The house dry wink only; no lampshading.
3. **No anachronism inside the sensitive frame.** Bwa Kayiman's documented-history treatment — and any family-opt-in Vodou content — is played straight. No phones in the ceremony scene.
4. **In-fiction the network is Fil la.** No real-world brand names in scenes. The family's real chat and voice notes stay real-life missions; the fiction mirrors them — that is the point.
5. **Modern voices, period stakes.** The enemy's intelligence channel is anachronistic too (intercepted memos in English) and is always confidently wrong.

## 3. The campaign — 8 chapters = the 8 units

Each unit's theme, vocabulary scope, grammar, and gate (`03-language-program.md` §4) stay exactly as specced; the chapter wraps them in the milestone. Chapter gating follows unit gates, never the calendar.

Every chapter carries: the milestone (below, with facts), the unit's language theme, an **anthem slot** (one modern Haitian track — boys nominate, family vets, chorus keys pre-taught; linked, never hosted), **enemy-intel beats** (GM-voiced intercepted memos, comic-overconfident), and a **signature voice-dispatch moment** (the chapter's set-piece spoken transmission).

## 4. The milestones — verified facts (binding)

### Ch1 · Bwa Kayiman + the August 1791 uprising
- **Kreyòl:** Bwa Kayiman · **French:** Bois Caïman · **Date:** ceremony traditionally the night of Aug 14, 1791 (some historians say ~Aug 21); the coordinated uprising across the northern plain began ~Aug 22, 1791.
- **Teen-facing fact:** An enslaved leader named Boukman led a secret night gathering where captives planned a coordinated revolt; within days, plantations across the north rose together. Remembered as the spark of the revolution.
- **People:** Dutty Boukman (Kreyòl: Boukman Dutty), a houngan; the mambo Cécile Fatiman is named in tradition.
- **Legend vs documented:** The uprising is documented. That a meeting/ceremony happened is accepted by most historians, but the vivid details (ritual specifics, Fatiman's role, Boukman's prayer) come from later secondhand accounts — treat specifics as tradition. **This is the religiously sensitive milestone** — chapter naming is Scott's open decision (opt-in "Bwa Kayiman" vs fallback **"Soulèvman 1791"**); the scene renders documented history only, ritual specifics omitted, **no anachronism inside the ceremony frame**.
- **Anthem-slot note:** Boukman Eksperyans is the natural candidate (the band is named for Boukman) — family vets; some of their catalog carries Vodou content, so track selection is the vetting question, not a formality.

### Ch2 · 1793 — Freedom proclaimed
- **Kreyòl person:** Tousen Louvèti · **Date:** Aug 29, 1793 (Sonthonax's emancipation proclamation in the North); France-wide abolition followed Feb 4, 1794.
- **Fact:** French commissioner Sonthonax declared the enslaved people of the north free — conceding on paper what the fighters had already taken. Toussaint Louverture rose as the revolution's general. (Alliance nuance for the game: Toussaint initially fought alongside the Spanish, and joined France only after abolition — the side that ends slavery is the side he takes.)
- **Documented:** solid.

### Ch3 · 1798 — The British defeated
- **Date:** 1798 (British evacuation; Môle-Saint-Nicolas handover).
- **Fact:** Toussaint out-generaled a full British invasion and sent them home without their colony. (Alt milestone if preferred: the 1801 Constitution, July 7, 1801 — abolished slavery permanently, made Toussaint governor-for-life, and provoked Napoleon.)
- **Documented:** solid.

### Ch4 · Ravine-à-Couleuvres — Feb 23, 1802
- **Kreyòl:** Ravin Koulèv (or Ravin-a-Kolèv) — **LOWER CONFIDENCE spelling, confirm with the native reviewer**; the French form is standard in writing.
- **Fact:** Toussaint's outnumbered soldiers made a fierce stand in a narrow wooded gorge against Napoleon's veteran invasion force — a tactical French win remembered as a heroic stand. Keep troop numbers vague (sources vary).
- **People:** Toussaint Louverture; opposing: Rochambeau under the Leclerc expedition.
- **Documented.**

### Ch5 · Crête-à-Pierrot — March 1802
- **Kreyòl:** Lakrèt-a-Pyewo (confirmed) · **Date:** siege ~March 4–24, 1802.
- **Fact:** Dessalines and a small force held a mountain fort about twenty days against a much larger French army, then cut their way out through the siege lines at night to fight on. Heroic extras (gore-free): Dessalines' rally — "let those who want to die free line up around me" (traditional wording, varies by source) — and Marie-Jeanne Lamartinière, a woman who fought among the defenders.
- **People:** Jean-Jacques Dessalines (Desalin); Marie-Jeanne Lamartinière.
- **Documented** (speech wording traditional).

### Ch6 · Congress of Arcahaie — May 18, 1803 — the flag
- **Kreyòl:** Akayè; Kongrè Akayè. May 18 = Jou Drapo (Flag Day).
- **Fact:** Rival leaders united and created the blue-and-red flag by tearing the white out of the French tricolor — Black and mixed-race Haitians sewn together. **The unity centerpiece — this is the "L'union fait la force" chapter.**
- **People:** Dessalines; Catherine Flon.
- **Legend vs documented:** the congress and the flag's creation are documented; **the story that Catherine Flon sewed it is cherished tradition, not attested in primary sources** — present with "the story goes."

### Ch7 · Vertières — Nov 18, 1803
- **Kreyòl:** Batay Vètyè (confirmed via Kreyòl Wikipedia); Capois = Kapwa, byname **Capois-la-Mò**.
- **Fact:** The last great battle; Napoleon's army beaten. Capois-La-Mort's horse was shot from under him — he got up and kept charging, shouting **« An avan! An avan! »** ("Forward! Forward!"). The story goes that Rochambeau briefly halted fire to salute his bravery (semi-legendary — frame as story).
- **Quote correction (binding):** the phrase "the bullets are dust" is **not attested** — do not use it. « An avan! An avan! » is the celebrated, attested cry.
- **People:** François Capois; Dessalines commanding; Rochambeau (French).
- **Battle and Capois' heroism documented.**

### Ch8 · Jan 1, 1804 — Independence at Gonaïves (+ the Citadelle)
- **Kreyòl:** Gonayiv; Endepandans Ayiti; Sitadèl Laferyè (confirmed; "Sitadèl Anri" also used — **pick one with the reviewer**). Citadelle built ~1805–1820, UNESCO World Heritage.
- **Fact:** Dessalines proclaimed the first nation born of a successful slave revolution, reclaiming the Indigenous name **Ayiti**. Henri Christophe (Anri Kristòf) then built the mountaintop Citadelle to guard the freedom — against a French return that never came.
- **Documented.** Caution: the proclamation itself contains vengeance rhetoric — paraphrase toward freedom and nation-building.
- **Ending beat:** after the celebration, the network stands down — and the credits name its members: the family. The reveal is not a twist; it's the thesis said out loud.

## 5. The motto

- **« L'union fait la force »** — confirmed as the motto on Haiti's coat of arms; the exact French phrasing is what every Haitian recognizes on sight. **Lead with the French form.** (The 1987 constitutional motto is technically "Liberté, Égalité, Fraternité"; the arms motto is the one Haitians universally treat as theirs.)
- Kreyòl rendering "Inite se fòs" exists but is far less iconic — fine as a gloss, never the headline. ("L'inyon fè lafòs" is not a standard form; skip it.)
- **Pair it with « Men anpil, chay pa lou »** — the go-to Kreyòl unity proverb, already the app's motto. The pairing is the co-op layer's voice.

## 6. Chapter anthems

One modern Haitian track per chapter. Rules:
- **Linked, never hosted.** The anthem plays from the family's own accounts/apps; the game shows the title, the chorus keys, and the drop moment.
- **Boys nominate, family vets** — every pick passes the family listen-through (`08-family-handbook.md` §Kickoff). Vodou-content tracks follow the same opt-in rule as everything else.
- **Chorus vocabulary is pre-taught** via the unit's song-keys strand (`03-language-program.md` §4) — the anthem is a text, not just a vibe.
- **The drop is a milestone event:** the anthem lands when the chapter's battle is won (an async GM move can deliver it as a commendation from command).
- Candidate pool from the vetted resources list: Boukman Eksperyans, RAM, Tabou Combo, Boukan Ginen, BIC ("Yon lèt pou ti jèn"), plus the boys' family-vetted modern nominations. No track is final until vetted; this corpus assigns **slots, not songs**.

## 7. Enemy intelligence (the GM's second voice)

Intercepted French/British/Spanish memos arrive on the network — in English (French appears only as historical artifact). The register is fixed by the copy law: **overconfident and out of their depth, never menacing.** The enemy is always certain, always slightly behind, and constitutionally unable to read Kreyòl — the boys' comprehension is dramatized as the thing the empire cannot buy. Historical enemy figures (Leclerc, Rochambeau) may be named and quoted-in-paraphrase consistent with §4's facts; invented enemy memos must contradict no documented event.

## 8. Memwa flashbacks (opt-in interludes — "the earlier bits")

Optional scenes unlocked between chapters, delivered through Grann's testimony (the elder of the network). Candidates — **NOT yet fact-checked; run a verification pass like this document's before writing any scene:**
- **Ayiti before 1492:** the Taíno, the name Ayiti ("land of mountains" — resonates with Dèyè mòn gen mòn), cacica **Anacaona** (executed 1503 — her poems/legacy are beloved; verify details before use).
- **Marronage:** escaped-slave communities in the mountains — the literal origin of "retreat to the mountains and return," the game's failure mechanic.
- **Makandal** (1750s): famous but Vodou-entangled — same family-opt-in class as Bwa Kayiman.
- **Middle Passage material: handle with extreme care or leave to the family; not a game level.**

## 9. Content red lines (binding)

1. **No gore, no atrocity content.** The 1804 massacre and vengeance rhetoric stay out; heroic-stand framing throughout; enemies are defeated, retreated, sent home — never slaughtered on screen. Dessalines is presented through leadership and heroism; the harder history is a family conversation, not an app card.
2. **Vodou is family-opt-in, never default.** Gran Bwa (a lwa) is out unless the family opts in; **Bwa Kayiman renders as documented history** (the secret meeting that sparked the uprising), ritual specifics omitted; Manman gets the explicit toggle; fallback chapter name "Soulèvman 1791."
3. **Legend labeled as legend** in-game: "the story goes…" for Flon's needle and Rochambeau's salute.
4. **French appears only as historical artifact** (the motto, names); the game teaches Kreyòl; capstone French asides belong to Manman.
5. **Register:** dry, wry, heroic-facts. The game must survive the "skeptical 15-year-old over his brother's shoulder" test AND the Manman veto.

## 10. Scene-text language rules (how narrative obeys the language law)

Scene narration is a **content class**, governed like feed posts — NOT chrome:
- Narration is English by default (comprehension scaffolding), with embedded Kreyòl dialogue/quotes as glossed content: every Kreyòl word ∈ scope ∪ glossed (`data-gloss` tap affordance), long-press reveals English.
- Chrome around scenes (buttons, headers, HUD) routes through the gate helpers with lawful computed tw values — zero Kreyòl literals in templates.
- **Every Claude-drafted Kreyòl string ships `needsReview:true` and is filtered from the boys' build until Manman passes it.** Claude coins zero words and zero slang; doubtful vocabulary ships English with a Manman ticket.
- In-fiction chat messages (the anachronistic texting layer) are the same content class: scope-constrained, glossed, reviewed. The U4 chat-register mini-unit teaches the register explicitly.
- Kreyòl density in scenes rises with the units (flood-before-focus): by Ch7–8, dialogue is mostly Kreyòl with English long-press as the escape hatch.
