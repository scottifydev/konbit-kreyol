# 02 · GAME SYSTEMS — mechanics

**Everything here is a shell around the pedagogy in `03-language-program.md`. Game layer ≠ learning layer: narrative never gates SRS mastery, and mastery displays never lie for drama. XP = effort; battle thresholds key off evidence-weighted mastery. If a mechanic here appears to fight that, the mechanic loses.**

---

## 1. The voice-dispatch loop (the spine)

Kreyòl is primarily a spoken language, so the game's central act is speaking it and being understood. One dispatch:

1. **Prompt.** A scene, mission, or battle asks the **sender** to record a spoken dispatch on his phone — a password, an order, a description, a war story. Prompts target specific scope items. Hold-to-record; pointer events; `touch-action:none`.
2. **Transit.** The dispatch travels the network. In-fiction, a runner carries it through the lines; GM and enemy-intel beats may reference dispatches in transit.
3. **Receipt and action.** The **receiver** (the other brother, or an adult) listens and must **act on it**: execute the order in-game, answer the embedded question, pick the move the message dictates. **Comprehension is proven by action, never by self-report** — there is no "mark understood" button that credits anything.
4. **Ledger writes, both directions.** One successful transmission = a production event for the sender and a listening-recognition event for the receiver, on the items the prompt targeted. The two-ledger model (`03` §6) maps onto the mechanic one-to-one.
5. **Failure blames the wire, never a boy.** An un-understood message renders as **garbled in transit — send it again**. The receiver can request a repeat; asking is authentic conversational repair (an exit-criterion behavior, `03` §12), not a penalty. No blame surface exists on either side; two boys' misses are never compared.
6. **Family feedback.** Adults on the network reply **by voice, recast style** — say it back naturally, never correct mid-sentence, never mock accent (the error-response brief as voice etiquette, `08` §3). Manman's commendations attach to dispatches. Boys' own recordings are **not** gated by the Cipher Office — the native gate protects what is shown *to* the boys; their productions get feedback, not filtering.
7. **Async-tolerant.** The daily loop never blocks on the other brother: pending dispatches queue, and "while you wait" content fills the gap. When both boys are on the same day, battles use live-ish voice volleys.
8. **Naming.** The mechanic ships under English chrome ("dispatch," "voice note") until Manman's naming/scope pass — "vwadyo" is currently an unlawful whitelist entry (`04` §Lint).

**Anti-gaming notes (design-normative):** receiver checks are keyed to message content (the right action is only knowable by understanding the audio); the sender's prompt and the receiver's check are generated as a pair; a receiver who guesses wrong twice triggers "garbled," which costs neither boy anything but writes no false evidence. Adults can flag a transmission "credited generously" in the GM console; that flag informs Manman's attention, never a ledger.

## 2. The daily loop — existing screens are the game verbs

The 30-minute daily loop (15-minute minimum viable session preserves streak + SRS) survives intact. **Surface names (Fil la, Fokis, Pwodiksyon, Mòn, Misyon, Anrejistrè) stay** — they are taught vocabulary with computed tw values, not renameable branding. The game frame is narrative dressing, not renaming.

| Activity | Game frame | Mechanics (unchanged core) |
|---|---|---|
| **Fil la** (feed) | The network's chat — messages from camp, family, the front, the enemy's intercepted memos | SRS reviews hidden in the scroll; 60/25/15 composer; reactions/Konprann write receptive reviews; due posts marked by the quiet sun dot |
| **Fokis** (slate) | The war college — an officer chalks the day's code on the board | Judgment-first grammar; additive chalk correction (circle the right answer, neutral strike, never red X) |
| **Pwodiksyon** (recorder) | Dispatch drills — passwords, orders read aloud, rallying lines; feeds the real dispatch pipeline | Hold-to-record vs family reference audio; production ledger; on phones this IS the dispatch composer |
| **The relay** (battles) | The chapter's engagement — each brother runs one leg; volleys = checks from HIS due queue; combined score vs threshold **takes the position** | Mandatory tip at handoff = passing the word down the line; XP effort-only; the position threshold keys off evidence-weighted mastery |
| **Misyon Fanmi** | Orders from the General's council (the family) | Manman's commendations gate missions; Grann interview = "record the elder's testimony" (becomes Ch5 listening) |
| **Dashboard** | The front page of the revolution's broadsheet: campaign map strip (8 milestones, current one live), Kle 77 tick-ruler, konbit board | Front-page IA per `05`; pending-dispatch tray |

Daily session order (`03` §5): Revi → Fil la → Fokis → Pwodiksyon/dispatches → Mòn/Konbit → daily micro-mission (say today's phrase to someone in the house; U4+: send one voice note).

## 3. Knowledge as the weapon — check taxonomy

1. **Recognition checks** (rec ledger): read the intercepted order, match the overheard word, act on your brother's dispatch. The game never blocks on words not yet taught or due.
2. **Production checks** (prod ledger): say the password, record the rally line, send the dispatch that your brother can act on. Graded by transmission and family feedback only — **no machine judgment, ever**.
3. **History checks:** milestone facts earn tactical choices ("You know Rochambeau's men hold the ravine — go around"). History checks are **bonus routes, never gates**: a boy who missed the fact still wins by language; knowing history makes it cooler, not possible. History checks never test anachronistic content as fact.
4. **Judgment checks** (Fokis): grammar-by-ear volleys during battles.

## 4. Battles and the co-op law (critical)

- **The brothers NEVER oppose each other.** The enemy is the historical army; a shared enemy strengthens the co-op frame. No PvP, no versus mode, no per-brother comparison on shared surfaces. Both **hold the position together** or the campaign waits. **« L'union fait la force » is the game's thesis**, rendered on konbit surfaces paired with « Men anpil, chay pa lou » (lead with the French form — it's the coat-of-arms phrasing every Haitian recognizes).
- **Failure = marronage, not game-over.** A lost battle **falls back to the mountains** and returns tomorrow with each other's tips — which is historically how the maroons and the revolution actually fought (« Dèyè mòn gen mòn » — behind mountains, more mountains; the refuge is endless). Sunset panorama, nobody-loses semantics. No lives, no game-over screen, no lost progress, ever. "Nobody loses" is said exactly once, at the battle-screen intro. *(This is marronage — falling back to the mountains to fight another day — never a failed "summit"; the climb metaphor is purged, `04` §2.10.)*
- **Relay structure:** each boy runs a leg matched to his Majò role (**Majò lekti / Majò koute**, swapping Mondays — each leads his *stronger* skill and gets coached in his weaker; protégé effect); volleys draw from his own due queue; the **mandatory tip at handoff** (Pass button disabled until the tip box has text — enforcement by button, not parenthesis) is retrieval for the writer, a hint for the reader. **Voice volleys:** where both boys play same-day, a battle leg can require a spoken dispatch the other must act on; the spoken handoff tip is a first-class option.
- **Konbit streak** survives if either brother plays; **padon** repairs are finite, counted, and honest. Individual streaks exist quietly in each boy's own masthead.

## 5. The GM layer — the adults are in the game

Every human-in-the-loop function has a diegetic identity, a surface, and agency. All GM surfaces are **adult surfaces** (parent-brief class — the kid copy law does not apply there; spec vocabulary is legal).

| Person | In-fiction role | Real function |
|---|---|---|
| Manman | **The Cipher Office** — high command's cryptographer; nothing goes out on the network until she certifies it's genuine | The native review gate (text queue) + the voice-feedback desk (recast replies to dispatches) + commendations (mission stars) |
| Scott | **The GM** — the war room, and the enemy's intelligence channel | Campaign pacing, async events, missions, enemy-intel memos, media/anthem vetting |
| Grann | **The elder** — her testimony unlocks the Memwa flashbacks | Recorded interview (U5 listening texts); reference audio |
| Cousins/uncles/aunts | **Field agents** | Slang slots, feed donations, audio, occasional dispatch recipients |

**Code names:** coined by the family at kickoff (`08` §5) — Claude drafts zero names. Boys may opt adults to "themselves" if code names don't stick.

**The GM console** (adult profiles; see `06` for schema, `08` for the guide):
- **Dispatch composer** — template-first, never a blank compose box. Drop a message into the boys' feed as command, a field agent, or the enemy channel. Any adult-authored Kreyòl except Manman's queues for her pass; her own is auto-certified (she *is* the gate).
- **Cipher Office queue** — the ht review queue as an in-game surface: certify, edit, or bounce each string; batch view; the escalation list from `08` §4 pinned.
- **Voice-feedback desk** — listen to the boys' dispatches; reply by voice (recast etiquette on-screen as a reminder, adult-facing).
- **Commendations** — mission stars and dispatch commendations, issued from her own logged-in door (replaces "hand her the mouse").
- **Event scheduler (async only)** — queue an ambush scene, a Memwa unlock, an enemy memo, an anthem drop at a milestone. Events land between sessions or at scene boundaries, never mid-scene.

**GM laws (binding — restated in `04`):**
- (a) **GM moves touch the narrative, never the mastery math.** No lever changes SRS state, battle thresholds, or mastery displays.
- (b) **Adults are never the enemy mechanically.** The fiction opposes the boys; the family never does. The enemy-intel channel is authored by Scott but the boys play against the historical army, not against Dad.
- (c) **Async only.** No live injection into a running scene.
- (d) **Kreyòl authorship rules apply to adults too** — everything except Manman's own words passes her gate before rendering to the boys.

## 6. Tone

Understated stats, dry copy, zero confetti, no mascot. **Pwo mode** per profile hides XP entirely (mastery grid + mountain only). Boys author feed posts and dispatches *for each other*; Kreyòl text posts pass the same native gate. The stakes-without-fear trick: the *revolution* has stakes; the *boys* only ever have tomorrow.
