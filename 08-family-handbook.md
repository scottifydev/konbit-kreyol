## 2. Scott's GM guide

**Your two voices:**
- **Command** — orders, missions, commendation notes, anthem drops. Warm, dry, brief.
- **Enemy intelligence** — intercepted French/British memos, in English. The register is fixed by law: **overconfident and out of their depth, never menacing.** The enemy is certain, pompous, and always slightly behind; his defining trait is that he cannot read Kreyòl and won't admit it matters. If a memo would scare a kid rather than make him grin, it fails.

**Your levers (all async — moves land between sessions or at scene boundaries, never mid-scene):**
- Drop a dispatch or memo into the feed (template-first composer).
- Schedule an event: an ambush scene, a Memwa unlock, an anthem drop on a milestone.
- Issue missions and commendations.
- What you can never do, by law and by schema: change SRS state, battle thresholds, or any mastery display. You run the war, not the gradebook.

**Cadence that works:** 2–3 moves a week beats daily noise. React to what the boys actually sent — an enemy memo that misquotes the dispatch Leo garbled yesterday ("the rebel code remains unbreakable; my analysts assure me it is merely bad French") is worth ten generic ones.

**Your Kreyòl:** anything you author in Kreyòl queues for Manman like everyone else's.

**Your open decisions (also in `00` §5):** Bwa Kayiman naming default; DUE-badge + scope-amendment sign-offs; the error-response one-pager sign-off before Unit 1; anthem shortlist; Supabase project setup.

## 3. The error-response rules (the single most critical safeguard — now also voice-reply etiquette)

Signed off before Unit 1, and they govern every adult voice reply on the network:

1. **Recast, don't correct mid-sentence.** Say it back naturally in your own words; don't stop him mid-stream.
2. **Never mock accent.** Ridicule from fluent relatives is the documented silencer of heritage learners; no feature of this program survives it.
3. **Praise attempts specifically** — name the thing he did right, not a generic "good job."
4. On the network this means: reply to a dispatch **by voice**, model the natural form inside your reply, and never open with the mistake.

The printable parent brief (from the Misyon issue) carries these plus Manman's weekly role rotation (judge / interviewee / co-player), the track explanation, and how the review queue works.

## 5. Kickoff agenda (one sitting, before Unit 1)

1. **The why**, said plainly: understand the room, voice-note Grann, get the jokes. Not school.
2. **The capacity contract:** 30 min/day, units-not-weeks, 15-minute minimum days count. Who does what beyond Mom: Grann/uncles record, aunt reviews, cousins feed slang.
3. **Code names:** the family coins the adults' network identities (and the boys', if they want them). Nothing sticks unless the boys think it's cool — "themselves" is a fine outcome.
4. **Anthem nominations:** the boys nominate tracks/creators (2–3 each); the family listen-through vets them; candidate pool in `03` §12. Chorus keys from the picks feed the units' song-key strand.
5. **Capstone format:** the boys pick how the Ch8 lodyans evening runs; Manman names it.
6. **Error-response one-pager:** read aloud once, signed.
7. **Schedule the 90-minute recording session** (floor — Units 1–2 reference audio). Until it happens, the app says "coming" on every empty slot, honestly.

## 6. Graceful degradation (honest planning)

If Mom's capacity drops below plan, the program degrades but doesn't lie: unreviewed Kreyòl stays English-side rather than shipping unreviewed; dispatch feedback falls back to transmission-only grading; missions thin before the daily loop does. The one thing with no fallback is the error-response rules — they cost nothing and protect everything.
