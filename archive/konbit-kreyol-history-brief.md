# KONBIT KREYÒL — Verified History Brief (the content bible for the Kanpay)
**Fact-checked 2026-07-10 via web sources (cited below). Kreyòl spellings follow IPN orthography; two are flagged lower-confidence for the native reviewer. Every "the story goes" flag is load-bearing: the game must not present tradition as documented fact.**

---

## The eight campaign milestones (chronological)

### 1 · Bwa Kayiman + the August 1791 uprising
- **Kreyòl:** Bwa Kayiman · **French:** Bois Caïman · **Date:** ceremony traditionally the night of Aug 14, 1791 (some historians say ~Aug 21); the coordinated uprising across the northern plain began ~Aug 22, 1791.
- **Teen-facing fact:** An enslaved leader named Boukman led a secret night gathering where captives planned a coordinated revolt; within days, plantations across the north rose together. Remembered as the spark of the revolution.
- **People:** Dutty Boukman (Kreyòl: Boukman Dutty), a houngan; the mambo Cécile Fatiman is named in tradition.
- **Legend vs documented:** The uprising is documented. That a meeting/ceremony happened is accepted by most historians, but the vivid details (ritual specifics, Fatiman's role, Boukman's prayer) come from later secondhand accounts — treat specifics as tradition. **This is the religiously sensitive milestone — see sensitivity notes.**

### 2 · 1793 — Freedom proclaimed
- **Kreyòl person:** Tousen Louvèti · **Date:** Aug 29, 1793 (Sonthonax's emancipation proclamation in the North); France-wide abolition followed Feb 4, 1794.
- **Fact:** French commissioner Sonthonax declared the enslaved people of the north free — conceding on paper what the fighters had already taken. Toussaint Louverture rose as the revolution's general. (Alliance nuance for the game: Toussaint initially fought alongside the Spanish, and joined France only after abolition — the side that ends slavery is the side he takes.)
- **Documented:** solid.

### 3 · 1798 — The British defeated
- **Date:** 1798 (British evacuation; Môle-Saint-Nicolas handover).
- **Fact:** Toussaint out-generaled a full British invasion and sent them home without their colony. (Alt milestone if preferred: the 1801 Constitution, July 7, 1801 — abolished slavery permanently, made Toussaint governor-for-life, and provoked Napoleon.)
- **Documented:** solid.

### 4 · Ravine-à-Couleuvres — Feb 23, 1802
- **Kreyòl:** Ravin Koulèv (or Ravin-a-Kolèv) — **LOWER CONFIDENCE spelling, confirm with the native reviewer**; the French form is standard in writing.
- **Fact:** Toussaint's outnumbered soldiers made a fierce stand in a narrow wooded gorge against Napoleon's veteran invasion force — a tactical French win remembered as a heroic stand. Keep troop numbers vague (sources vary).
- **People:** Toussaint Louverture; opposing: Rochambeau under the Leclerc expedition.
- **Documented.**

### 5 · Crête-à-Pierrot — March 1802
- **Kreyòl:** Lakrèt-a-Pyewo (confirmed) · **Date:** siege ~March 4–24, 1802.
- **Fact:** Dessalines and a small force held a mountain fort about twenty days against a much larger French army, then cut their way out through the siege lines at night to fight on. Heroic extras (gore-free): Dessalines' rally — "let those who want to die free line up around me" (traditional wording, varies by source) — and Marie-Jeanne Lamartinière, a woman who fought among the defenders.
- **People:** Jean-Jacques Dessalines (Desalin); Marie-Jeanne Lamartinière.
- **Documented** (speech wording traditional).

### 6 · Congress of Arcahaie — May 18, 1803 — the flag
- **Kreyòl:** Akayè; Kongrè Akayè. May 18 = Jou Drapo (Flag Day).
- **Fact:** Rival leaders united and created the blue-and-red flag by tearing the white out of the French tricolor — Black and mixed-race Haitians sewn together. **The unity centerpiece — this is the "L'union fait la force" chapter.**
- **People:** Dessalines; Catherine Flon.
- **Legend vs documented:** the congress and the flag's creation are documented; **the story that Catherine Flon sewed it is cherished tradition, not attested in primary sources** — present with "the story goes."

### 7 · Vertières — Nov 18, 1803
- **Kreyòl:** Batay Vètyè (confirmed via Kreyòl Wikipedia); Capois = Kapwa, byname **Capois-la-Mò**.
- **Fact:** The last great battle; Napoleon's army beaten. Capois-La-Mort's horse was shot from under him — he got up and kept charging, shouting **« An avan! An avan! »** ("Forward! Forward!"). The story goes that Rochambeau briefly halted fire to salute his bravery (semi-legendary — frame as story).
- **Quote correction (binding):** the phrase "the bullets are dust" is **not attested** — do not use it. « An avan! An avan! » is the celebrated, attested cry.
- **People:** François Capois; Dessalines commanding; Rochambeau (French).
- **Battle and Capois' heroism documented.**

### 8 · Jan 1, 1804 — Independence at Gonaïves (+ the Citadelle)
- **Kreyòl:** Gonayiv; Endepandans Ayiti; Sitadèl Laferyè (confirmed; "Sitadèl Anri" also used — pick one with the reviewer). Citadelle built ~1805–1820, UNESCO World Heritage.
- **Fact:** Dessalines proclaimed the first nation born of a successful slave revolution, reclaiming the Indigenous name **Ayiti**. Henri Christophe (Anri Kristòf) then built the mountaintop Citadelle to guard the freedom — against a French return that never came.
- **Documented.** Caution: the proclamation itself contains vengeance rhetoric — paraphrase toward freedom and nation-building.

## The motto
- **« L'union fait la force »** — confirmed as the motto on Haiti's coat of arms; the exact French phrasing is what every Haitian recognizes on sight. **Lead with the French form.** (The 1987 constitutional motto is technically "Liberté, Égalité, Fraternité"; the arms motto is the one Haitians universally treat as theirs.)
- Kreyòl rendering "Inite se fòs" exists but is far less iconic — fine as a gloss, never the headline. ("L'inyon fè lafòs" is not a standard form; skip it.)
- **Pair it with « Men anpil, chay pa lou »** — the go-to Kreyòl unity proverb, already the app's motto. The pairing is the co-op layer's voice.

## Sensitivity rulings (binding on all content)
1. **Gran Bwa** is primarily a Vodou lwa (forest spirit, petro; healing/initiation). It reads as religious Vodou content to a Protestant Haitian family. **Not used as a theme or challenge name unless the family opts in** — this applies the design constitution's existing Cycle-2 rule (religious motifs: default out, family opt-in) to new material.
2. **Bwa Kayiman controversy is real and documented:** since the 1990s some evangelical/Protestant currents recast the ceremony as a "pact with the devil" (Vision Haiti's 1998 march; Pat Robertson's 2010 remarks). Many diaspora Protestant families view it negatively. **Ruling:** the game presents it strictly as documented history — the secret planning meeting that sparked the revolution — with ritual specifics left out; Manman gets an explicit opt-in/opt-out on naming the site, with "Soulèvman 1791" as the fallback chapter name.
3. **Out of a celebration frame entirely:** the 1804 massacre of remaining French colonists (documented, ~3,000–7,000, on Dessalines' orders); the vengeance rhetoric of the independence declaration; all gore. Dessalines is presented through leadership and heroism; the harder history is a family conversation, not an app card.
4. **French in the game** appears only as historical artifact (the motto, place names); the capstone's French asides belong to Manman, per the master spec.
